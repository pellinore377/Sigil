# Group implementation contract — draft

Status: experimental local membership, Sender Keys, optional same-server private authority and durable native service adapter. Item 2 remains incomplete; this is not a reviewed cryptographic composition. This develops the requirements in [GroupsFederation.md](GroupsFederation.md). The existing pairwise Triple Ratchet remains unchanged. No new anonymous-credential proof system is authorized by this document.

## Authority and identity

A group is identified by a random genesis nonce and the digest of its complete authenticated genesis. Device identities are full signed-binding fingerprints, not display names, transport credentials or mutable homeserver directory entries. Member references are group-local stable identifiers. A member's authorized devices are enumerated explicitly; account access and a newly linked device do not implicitly expand that list.

An invitation names the group, exact predecessor, candidate member and proposed device bindings. Acceptance proves possession of the invited device identity and consent to that group. An existing administrator authorizes membership against the predecessor. Transport authentication is never sufficient. Other members learn new device bindings through an authenticated group transition, without silently upgrading those devices to independently verified personal contacts.

Each state contains a revision, predecessor digest, members with roles and authorized device fingerprints, policy, and key epoch. Canonical member/device ordering is mandatory; duplicates and conflicting ownership are invalid. At least one administrator must remain in a live group. A closed group is explicit and terminal. A replacement never edits the identity behind an existing device fingerprint.

## Concurrent administrative changes

Candidate coordination: one group authority service serializes opaque proposals with compare-and-swap on the exact predecessor digest. An administrative signature authorizes the proposal; the service's receipt establishes ordering only. Neither can substitute for the other. The authority is pinned in genesis; authority migration requires an authenticated transition.

Two proposals for the same predecessor are not merged. One can commit; the other returns a conflict and requires fresh validation and fresh approval against the resulting state. Wall-clock timestamps never grant authority. Retried identical commits return the original receipt. A conflicting receipt is evidence of service equivocation, not permission to choose an arbitrary branch.

Clients retain authenticated predecessor/commit evidence. On observing contradictory signed authority receipts for the same predecessor they persist a frozen state and stop new sender-key distribution and group sends. A conflicting head or revision is contradictory ordering evidence. An unsigned proposal or forged receipt cannot freeze a group. Two valid authority signatures can establish service equivocation even if that service also failed membership authorization; freezing never authorizes the other proposal. Existing plaintext remains readable. Automatic fork resolution is excluded: explicit reconciliation must identify the accepted branch and rotate keys, or members establish a new group. The service can withhold a fork from isolated clients; this scheme detects observed equivocation and does not promise global instantaneous consistency against a malicious service.

## Epoch cutover and Sender Keys

Every committed state change advances the key epoch and binds it to the new state digest, including policy and role changes. Removal cannot retain an old sender chain for new sends. An offline sender must obtain and validate the current committed state before releasing a distribution. A stale client cannot be prevented from encrypting while isolated; delivery acceptance must enforce the current epoch and document availability assumptions.

For a local cutover, commit the new state and invalidate old outgoing epochs in one database transaction. New sender entropy and its recipient manifest are prepared durably for the exact new authorized set. A crash resumes the original distribution bytes and retry identifiers. Group ciphertext becomes eligible for fan-out only under its matching state and sender epoch. A missing recipient distribution remains explicit pending work.

Messages bind format version, group, state digest, epoch, sender fingerprint, sender-chain identifier, counter and logical message identifier. Distribution binds the same identity context and travels over authenticated pairwise channels. Never export live chains through history recovery. Counter advancement, ciphertext and immutable fan-out work must commit together. Receive key advancement and logical deduplication must commit together.

Delayed old-epoch traffic must not silently become a current-epoch message. Its eligibility requires authenticated evidence of the applicable historical membership and the chosen cutover rule; until this rule is implemented, reject/defer it explicitly. There is no trustworthy sender timestamp that proves a removed device sent before removal. Do not imply that removal revokes previously disclosed messages or key material.

Sender Keys require a separately pinned construction and test vectors before implementation. Pairwise post-quantum healing does not automatically make an exposed group sender chain heal. Fresh distribution only helps under its stated channel and authentication assumptions. The Sender Keys analysis is a research input, not a security proof of this composition. [Analysis, version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07045v2).

## Membership privacy and federation gate

The authority must not receive plaintext member identities or group attributes. Signal's credential design assumes a central service that both issues and verifies keyed anonymous credentials. Its federation adaptation is still a separate design choice. [Signal's explanation](https://signal.org/blog/signal-private-group-system/), [private-group paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1416).

The independent credential implementation below follows the published construction. Do not substitute a bearer token and label it equivalent to Signal private groups. Do not import Signal implementation code or implement a novel proof system. Enabling an authority endpoint still requires the issuer/federation integration described here. Development verification does not require an external reviewer on standby; the independent audit follows backend implementation completion. Routing metadata remains observable as documented in the plan.

## Earlier history

Default access begins at the accepted join revision. Earlier history requires an explicit administrator-authorized grant binding group, target member/device, permitted historical range and source device. The source verifies current authorization before preparing an encrypted history-only transfer. A transfer cannot contain live sender or pairwise state. Deleted, expired and view-once content is excluded. Missing source or content is pending/unavailable, not successful recovery. A later revocation cannot retract already received plaintext.

### Authority service and native integration — server 19 / native 55

The server exposes `GET/PUT /admin/v0/groups` behind Admin authentication. Initial configuration is disabled, revision zero, with a 1 GiB authority storage budget. Enable with `{"expected_revision":0,"enabled":true,"storage_limit_bytes":1073741824}`. The supported budget range is 1 MiB–1 TiB. It accounts for credential UID reservations, group/control/member records and replay nonces separately from account billing; it does not claim exact SQLite disk usage. Configuration retries retain the same signed issuer profile and keys.

`POST /client/v0/groups/credential` requires an active native device credential and its published signed binding. Issuance binds the exact profile, full identity fingerprint, derived UID and current UTC day. The issuer retains UID/full-fingerprint collision checks. `GET /groups/v0/authority` and `POST /groups/v0/request` reject account authorization headers, cookies, browser origins and query strings. These anonymous routes still use the global request/work budgets. Native HTTPS uses the configured homeserver and certificate roots, never follows a foreign authority URL and omits the device credential on anonymous requests.

An operation proof binds the exact canonical operation body, group, predecessor, profile, nonce and short deadline. Verification, roster authorization, CAS, replay admission, storage reservation and signed ordering evidence share one database transaction. Only a creator's own administrator ciphertext can create a group. Existing administrators submit all state changes, including independently signed member leave/device proposals. Member-to-administrator proposal relay remains unfinished; regular devices cannot directly advance the authority. This prevents a regular member from poisoning the opaque ordering head by supplying an invalid unchanged-roster or self-removal control. The server cannot validate the opaque proposal's member signatures: submitting administrators and receiving clients must do that. An administrator can still withhold or poison service availability; ordering is not end-to-end authorization.

Controls are bounded to 192 KiB, rosters to 1,024 sorted unique encrypted device UIDs and reads to two contiguous commits. Logical mutation retries use a fresh nonce/proof and retrieve the original receipt for the exact frozen operation, including after its author leaves. Another body at the same revision conflicts. Restore disables the service and marks existing groups read-only for new ordering; reenabling does not unfreeze those groups. Authority keys and their checkpoint wrapping material reside in the protected server database and its operator backups; their wrapping does not protect against database theft.

Native schema 55 stores the issuer profile/master and one retained submission slot per group under the client storage key. `pin_group_service` accepts an already authenticated invitation context and refuses a different master or issuer profile, including a same-generation signed fork. Each group requires its own fresh random master; invitation generation/distribution is still unfinished. `prepare_group_service_request` freezes a fully authorized encrypted genesis/proposal. `submit_group_service_online` obtains a credential, submits the original operation, validates the receipt and commits through the existing membership journal. If acknowledgement fails after local acceptance, retry returns the same authority evidence and the membership journal reports a duplicate. `sync_group_service_online` applies bounded authenticated pages and resolves a losing queued proposal as superseded before permitting a newly approved replacement. These live service keys are not exported as retained-history recovery.

Service-pinned outgoing distributions and group messages refresh the authority before network release and recheck the resulting local state. A newer epoch cancels old queued work. A failed read, removed membership or incomplete synchronization blocks release. This cannot recall in-flight packets or prevent another update immediately after a successful read. Groups without a pinned service remain the existing experimental local API; they do not acquire this online cutover guarantee.

Privacy limits remain explicit. The authority stores encrypted device identifiers and encrypted controls, but observes group handles, roles, roster sizes, timing and update ordering. Credentials already issued remain valid through their UTC day; removing a ciphertext from the roster denies its subsequent reads, while transport credential revocation prevents future issuance. A retained group master is not revoked cryptographically by that access check. Existing mailbox group packets expose their Sender Key context and routing metadata; the combined mailbox/authority operator does not have a proved membership-unlinkability guarantee. Anonymous authority proofs alone do not finish transport privacy, federation adaptation or post-quantum group privacy.

Validation includes real HTTPS credential issuance and signed group changes, competing writers, exact lost receipts after administrator self-removal/restart, injected database failures, UID collisions, configuration retries, API access boundaries, immutable client pins, ciphertext record transplants, concurrent administrator proposals and queued epoch cutover. ### Independent authentication credentials — experimental profile 0

`crypto/src/private_credentials.rs` implements the algebraic MAC and issuer proof from sections 3.1–3.2, UID encryption from section 4.1, and combined authentication presentation from section 5.12 of the [private-group paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1416). The pinned PDF SHA-256 is `9205034e9d1448a5f020b7a78aea6290de005f311429cacc6781b1f544ca51fc`. No Signal implementation code was used. Existing curve25519-dalek, SHA-512 and secure randomness dependencies supply the primitives; no dependencies changed.

The profile uses ristretto255 and canonical 32-byte scalars. Group element derivation uses the existing RFC 9496 implementation. Parameter points derive independently from SHA-512 of `Sigil/private-credentials/v0 || u32be(label_length) || label`, followed by ristretto255 element derivation. Labels are `Gw`, `Gwprime`, `Gx0`, `Gx1`, `Gy1`–`Gy3`, `Gm3`, `GV`, `Ga1`, `Ga2`. No parameter is constructed by multiplying a known scalar by a shared base point.

UIDs are 16-byte issuer-scoped identifiers, not display names or implicit authorization. The first attribute hashes the label `UID || uid` through the same domain-separated element derivation. Reversible encoding places the UID at bytes 1–16 of a zeroed 32-byte candidate, with a little-endian 16-bit counter at bytes 17–18. Select the first canonical, nonidentity ristretto encoding while scanning counters 0–65535; fail explicitly if none exists. Decode checks reserved bytes and recomputes that first encoding. This is Sigil's bounded data encoding, not Signal's wire encoding or a hash-to-group replacement. Its search work is UID-dependent; it is not advertised as a constant-time plaintext encoder. UID decryption is a client-local operation, not a service oracle.

The issuer has seven independent secret scalars. Its public parameters are 64 bytes. An issuance response contains a scalar and two group elements followed by a generalized Schnorr proof (352 bytes total). Acceptance binds the pinned issuer, both UID attributes, a `u32` redemption day and caller-supplied issuance context. The issuer must authenticate the UID and enforce allowed dates; the primitive does not infer either from a request.

A 32-byte group master key derives two scalars using SHA-512 with the profile domain, `group-key`, the scalar index byte and the master bytes. A presentation contains eight points and a six-witness proof (480 bytes). It proves the certified identity matches the deterministic encrypted UID for the group parameters. Fresh randomness changes presentations while retaining the same within-group UID ciphertext. This does not hide within-group linkage, roster size, roles, network endpoints or timing. These credentials provide no post-quantum privacy claim.

Fiat–Shamir challenges hash the profile domain, proof kind, witness/equation counts, length-prefixed context, and every equation's ordered bases, target and commitment. Presentation context additionally hashes issuer parameters, group public parameters and day. Callers must bind the actual operation, authority, group, body digest and replay nonce and enforce nonce use durably. The verifier returns a proven UID ciphertext; it does not grant membership, enforce roles or prevent request replay by itself. Profile-key blind issuance is not implemented by this authentication credential API.

Issuer and accepted credential checkpoints use the existing authenticated storage primitive, distinct type/version headers and caller record binding. Restore checks expected issuer parameters; credential restore also checks UID and day. Group master keys and accepted credentials must never enter ordinary history recovery.

Five focused tests cover issuance, randomized presentations, independently generated C/libsodium fixtures, canonical reversible encoding, ciphertext authentication and encrypted checkpoint binding. Altered identities, issuer parameters, dates, groups, request contexts and every presentation/issuance byte are rejected. Full optimized crypto validation passes **95 tests, one existing ignored test**; Clippy with warnings denied passes. The test-only C generator passes ASan/UBSan and reproduces the committed fixture. Commands: `cargo test --locked -p sigil-crypto --release`; `cc -std=c11 -Wall -Wextra -Werror -fsanitize=address,undefined crypto/tests/private-credentials.c -lsodium -o /tmp/sigil-private-credentials-reference`; run the resulting binary and compare its JSON with `crypto/tests/vectors/private-credentials.json`. Logs: `/tmp/sigil-private-credential-crypto-release.log`, `/tmp/sigil-private-credential-clippy.log`.

This completes the first credential primitive increment, not the private authority service, the federation profile, or item 2. Independent C fixtures check implementation agreement; they are not a proof of Sigil's composition or an external audit.

`crypto/src/private_group.rs` adds signed authority profiles and canonical application contexts. Profiles bind a canonical server, nonzero credential generation, issuer parameters and the genesis-pinned signing identity. Profile IDs exclude randomized signatures. Issuance verifies the full signed device binding and derives an issuer-scoped 16-byte UID from its full fingerprint and authority signing identity; the service must retain collision checks and authenticate account admission separately. Request contexts bind the authority profile ID, operation, group, predecessor, canonical body digest, nonzero nonce and a deadline of at most 120 seconds. The client receipt code now shares the same authority-fingerprint implementation; the superseded function was removed.

## Local authorization increment

`sigil_client::groups` implements signed genesis with a pinned creator fingerprint, canonical membership, predecessor-bound administrative proposals and exact-device consent. Every invited device signs its specific proposal. Adding a device to an existing member additionally needs an existing device of that member to sign; an administrator's signature plus a server-asserted account binding cannot replace that consent. Members can leave and manage their own device list. Administration controls other membership and roles. A live group must retain an administrator. Closure is terminal. History access starts disabled.

State heads exclude randomized binding/approval signatures. They hash a domain-separated canonical state, predecessor and author; approvals sign a separate transcript binding group, predecessor, author and resulting head. Existing XEdDSA/SHA-256 primitives are reused. This is an experimental application authorization format, not a Signal group wire format or a claim of post-quantum authentication.

The current bounds are 256 members, 256 devices per member and 1,024 explicitly authorized devices per group. A control is at most 160 KiB; it is not inserted into the existing smaller direct-text envelope. Strict decoders rebuild a proposal through predecessor policy rather than accepting serialized approval requirements. A parsed incomplete proposal cannot pass authorization.

Validation: `cargo test --locked -p sigil-client --lib groups::` passes eight tests covering real signed joins through 256 members, missing/incorrect consent, account/device aliases, predecessor conflicts, cross-group signature substitution, role/removal/closure rules, strict framing and remote approval round trips. These are local acceptance tests for this increment only.

## Durable local ordering increment

Native schema 43 stores encrypted genesis, current membership, pending proposals, accepted controls/receipts and equivocation evidence. `create_group` creates local genesis only. `accept_group_genesis` requires a currently independently verified creator and does not add the recipient to membership. `prepare_group_change` and `approve_group_proposal` durably retain exact approvals; repeating an incomplete request merges with retained signatures instead of dropping them. Pending work can be enumerated after restart. At most 16 proposals are pending per group. Discarding a local queue entry does not revoke an already shared signature; only an intervening committed state invalidates its predecessor-bound approval.

`commit_group_proposal` requires all device approvals and an ordering receipt signed by the genesis-pinned authority key. It commits current membership, epoch and retained receipt/control together, then drops obsolete pending proposals in that same transaction. Exact committed retries return `Duplicate`. Historical ordering evidence remains retained under the database storage budget. Contradictory authority receipts atomically persist evidence and a freeze flag in encrypted current state; losing the evidence row cannot remove that flag. Preparation, approval and pending submission retrieval refuse a frozen group. Schema 44 adds live sender/receiver checkpoints and distribution cutover as described below.

The receipt signing primitive alone does not implement a service or compare-and-swap. These local journal tests deliberately issue synthetic receipts, including conflicting receipts. The service/native integration above additionally tests receipts issued by the real authority.

Large group controls and checkpoints use a bounded local chunk frame around existing `StorageKey` records. Every chunk authenticates the group/device/record context, a fresh random whole-value identifier, total length and chunk ordinal. The whole frame is committed as one database value. This preserves the existing primitive's size bound and rejects chunk splicing; it is not a new message cipher or a backup format.

New persistence checks cover join consent versus ordering, exact restart retries, transaction-failure rollback, authenticated fork freeze across restart, historical contradictory receipts, trust-gated genesis, checkpoint substitution, schema 42→43 migration, reusable pending capacity and a large roster/approval record exceeding one storage record. The large-roster test first reproduced `Crypto(Limit)` in the new persistence code and passes after chunk framing; ordinary small-group tests alone did not expose that limit.

## Durable key-distribution increment

Schema 44 adds encrypted sender/receiver checkpoints and private distribution journals. `prepare_group_distribution` freezes one recipient's ciphertext over an existing, independently verified same-server pairwise session. All recipients must belong to the exact committed group state. The sender becomes ready only when every authorized recipient's distribution is durably frozen; its separately stored initial seed is then removed. Readiness means local preparation, not remote installation. A repeated preparation returns the original transport ID.

The normal mailbox worker authenticates and installs distributions in the same transaction as pairwise advancement and acknowledgement bookkeeping. Ordinary inbox/outbox records retain only a keyed, non-secret receipt. Recovery history excludes the control entirely. Cached receipts cannot reset advanced receivers. Public low-level receive/initial APIs refuse fresh distribution controls so they cannot commit a half-installed group setup.

Membership cutover and fork freeze remove live group keys and cancel locally queued distributions in the same transaction. Already accepted or in-flight packets cannot be recalled. Retained receipts remain usable for exact acknowledgement retries without reviving old keys.

Four integration tests cover real HTTPS/mailbox delivery, both pairwise directions, restart, failed journal/retirement transactions, recovery exclusion, advanced-receiver retry, missing group-join approval and malformed seed storage. This increment requires existing independently verified sessions: group-scoped trust, automatic fresh-channel setup, distribution-loss recovery and earlier-history transfer remain unfinished. The later authority adapter does not remove those channel dependencies.

## Durable group-message increment

Schema 45 separates group ciphertext/history, recipient delivery jobs and inbound acknowledgements from pairwise session storage. `queue_group_text` atomically advances one sender chain and freezes one ciphertext with distinct recipient delivery IDs. An exact logical retry checks content and timestamp without advancing the chain, including after cutover; it never silently turns a cancelled old send into a new-epoch send. Pending capacity is 16 logical messages per group and is reusable after delivery, expiry or cancellation. Sole-member groups retain local messages without network jobs or exported seed material.

The normal worker submits at most 16 group jobs per pass, preserves the oldest pending message per group/recipient, and retains a durable scan cursor. Each target's distribution must first have a stored server receipt. One failed target does not authorize deleting the shared retry ciphertext. Completion validates authenticated job statuses before clearing it. Server acceptance followed by a failed local receipt write retries the exact original packet; acceptance is still not a read receipt.

Inbound group packets validate the verified sender, exact current membership, recipient transport ID, signature, AEAD and encrypted text binding. Receiver evolution, logical history, recovery retention and acknowledgement bookkeeping commit together. Reordered packets use bounded skipped keys; identical packets under another server sequence do not consume a key twice or duplicate logical history. Direct-message and recovery-control journals cannot claim an already committed group sequence. Exact acknowledgements for previously committed content remain valid after cutover; new old-state delivery fails.

`RefreshKeys` is an administrator-authorized, ordered group transition with unchanged membership and a fresh epoch. It does not rely on toggling a user setting. Fresh distributions use new entropy and chain IDs. Refresh, membership changes and authority-fork freeze cancel pending old-state group messages with key retirement. Traffic already in flight cannot be recalled. This is not a claim of group post-compromise recovery over an unhealed pairwise channel.

Seven integration tests cover real three-member fan-out, failed preparation/receive/receipt/cutover writes, restart, shared-ciphertext retention, tampered status hints, reorder/replay, fresh epochs/fork freeze, maximum text, schema 44→45 migration, reusable pending capacity and sole-member behavior. Retained ordinary group text enters the existing recovery archive under its authenticated author identity key; secret distributions do not. General conversation edits/deletions/expiry, earlier-history grants, distribution-loss recovery, group-scoped trust and private service coordination remain open.

## Typed file delivery (native schema 46)

Groups now carry the canonical file body through the same Sender Keys counter,
ciphertext, recipient-job and history transaction as ordinary text. Published-cache
handoffs require current group authorization and the same-server file source. The
normal mailbox emits `GroupFile`; download preparation reloads the authenticated
retained group record and respects its recovery tombstone. One integration test
checks failed fan-out/archive writes, real delivery and download, author mismatch
and tombstones. Recovery retains the explicit file content type. This does not
supply private group services or authorize access to earlier group history.
