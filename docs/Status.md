## Speed and verification

Notes no longer wait before first load or reopen through the regular timeline. Superseded searches cancel, same-query results remain during refresh, and browser scans yield between bounded batches. Native search enumerates active group memberships without reconstructing every group timeline per batch. Android posts bypass the UI refresh mutex while preserving single-flight posting and stable retry IDs; the selected timeline updates after durable local enqueue. Healthy outgoing work wakes persisted messaging/file schedules immediately without clearing failure backoff or active reservations. Packet-submit recipient 404s retain queued bytes but do not stop unrelated outbound delivery; prekey/authentication failures remain blocking. Independent source review found no concrete regression in these paths; this is not race-stress evidence.

Conversation history is preloaded rather than paged by hand. The core owns the depth: `timeline_preload` turns the viewport position the list reports into the number of messages the client should hold and the depth at which it must ask again, and the timeline reply carries both; the clients only render and report. The "Earlier messages" wall is gone. A scan publishes once it holds what the reader can already see, but never past the target the core set: returning to the latest messages drops the loaded history and the target with it, and without that second bound the scan would run out of pages having published nothing and strand the reader on history with no way back. The list drops its own depth mark when the history under it shrinks, so paging resumes after such a reset instead of waiting for a depth that is gone. The core also owns how deep the list keeps items composed, three screens towards older history and two towards the newest, reported with every timeline page, so attachments and cards are decoded before they are reached; items composed but not on screen are neither marked read nor animated, and each row derives its own visibility so that a scroll invalidates the rows that crossed the edge rather than every row the buffer holds. A viewport report while a load is running extends that load instead of restarting it, on both Android and the browser. A new message rises and fades into place over 200 ms under the reduced-motion policy, once per message and only while it is still new: one that arrived screens away from the reader is simply there when they reach it rather than replaying its rise as it scrolls past. Pictures and video posters fade into a frame already sized from the file's own header or metadata, read in steps of 16, 64 and 256 KiB and read again once a video's file has arrived so the poster cannot resize the frame under itself; a picture whose shape is not known yet holds a 4:3 height rather than a full-width square, and pending audio holds the height of the player it becomes. A key anchor keeps the reader's place when the message they are looking at is removed, and returning to the latest messages discards that anchor with the history it pointed into. The deeper buffer is not shown to reduce jank and that acceptance item is unmet: the only measurement on record held 158 messages against 64 before over six scripted scrolls, but frame time rose about 1 ms at every percentile (p50 8 to 9 ms, p90 10 to 12 ms, p99 13 to 15 ms) and dropped frames, 0.13-0.26% before against 0.15-0.56% after, did not separate from run-to-run noise.
